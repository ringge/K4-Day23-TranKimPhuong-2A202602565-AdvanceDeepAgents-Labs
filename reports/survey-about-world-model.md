# World Models in AI: From Latent Prediction to Actionable Simulation

## TL;DR
- “World model” is used for both internal representations of how an environment works and models that predict future states for simulation and decision-making [1][2].
- The main design split is between reconstruction-grounded recurrent dynamics, control-oriented latent prediction, and predictive representations that learn from video without reconstructing pixels [1][3][4][5].
- Generative video can look plausible without reliably predicting physical outcomes: controlled benchmarks report substantial gaps in physical reasoning and generalization beyond familiar cases [6][7][8].
- For embodied use, planning needs more than visual fidelity: evaluation should measure physics, instruction following, action utility, and performance under new conditions [9][7].

## Background
A world model is an internal, learned account of an environment used to predict what may happen, interpret its state, or support action selection. One recent survey distinguishes models intended to represent mechanisms from models intended to predict future states for simulation and decision-making [2]. The distinction matters because learning to generate a likely-looking next frame is not the same objective as preserving action-relevant state or forecasting consequences accurately. The field therefore includes both model-based reinforcement-learning systems and newer video-prediction and representation-learning approaches, whose metrics and use cases are not interchangeable [1][4][5][9].

## Latent dynamics: reconstructing state, predicting task-relevant futures
A foundational route is a recurrent latent state-space model: encode observations into a compact state, predict its evolution under actions, and use the learned dynamics for planning. PlaNet combines deterministic and stochastic transition components and trains with observation reconstruction; its latent-overshooting objective addresses multi-step prediction. Its authors report solving pixel-based control problems involving contact, partial observability, or sparse rewards with fewer episodes than model-free comparisons and similar or sometimes higher final performance on their evaluated tasks [1]. This supports latent simulation as a useful control tool in that benchmark, not as evidence of universal transfer.

DreamerV3 extends this family by learning an RSSM that predicts representations, reward, and continuation, then learning an actor and critic from imagined futures. Its HF record reports a fixed configuration across more than 150 tasks, including Minecraft diamond collection without human data or curricula [3]. TD-MPC2 instead uses a decoder-free, control-centric latent model trained around joint-embedding prediction, reward and value learning, and local trajectory optimization. The paper record and conference abstract report results on 104 online RL tasks and a 317M-parameter model trained across 80 tasks [4]. These results show distinct design priorities: reconstructive models retain an explicit observation-prediction path, while TD-MPC2 directly optimizes latent prediction for control. Reported control-suite results cannot be treated as an apples-to-apples ranking without shared data, compute, and task protocols [1][3][4].

## Predictive representations and video generation
A second route predicts representations rather than pixels. V-JEPA predicts embeddings for withheld video regions without pixel-level reconstruction, text, or negative examples; its authors report representation-transfer results on video and image benchmarks, including 81.9% top-1 on Kinetics-400 for the largest model [5]. This can avoid requiring the latent model to reproduce every visual detail, but the cited evidence is primarily representation evaluation, not action-conditioned planning or physical robot control [5].

Generative video models provide a complementary path: they predict or synthesize visually detailed sequences, potentially offering a simulation-like interface. However, appearance is not a sufficient proxy for physical prediction. Physics-IQ evaluates continuation of controlled videos across physical domains and reports that physical understanding was severely limited and unrelated to visual realism for the tested video models [6]. A separate 2D mechanics study reports strong in-distribution performance but failure on out-of-distribution scenarios, interpreting the observed generalization as case-based rather than abstraction of general physical rules; its claims are limited to the study’s testbed and models [7]. PhyGenBench likewise reports difficulty with physical commonsense in its curated prompts, particularly dynamic scenarios, and states that scale or prompt engineering alone did not resolve the tested challenges [8]. Together these studies motivate separating image quality from the ability to predict outcomes under physical variation [6][7][8].

## Planning and embodiment: from imagined futures to action
In model-based control, a learned model is useful when its predictions support choosing and revising actions, not merely generating a future sequence. PlaNet and TD-MPC2 use latent rollouts and planning for online continuous-control tasks [1][4]. Dreamer learns policies and value functions from imagined futures, making the model part of the policy-learning loop [3]. These approaches differ from passive video prediction in being trained and evaluated in terms that directly involve task reward or control [1][3][4].

Video world models are also being judged as possible action simulators. WorldModelBench evaluates video generation for instruction following and physics adherence, using human-labeled examples to tune an automated judge, dimensions that are distinct from a general video-quality assessment [9]. Thus a strong perceptual score alone does not establish suitability for policy evaluation or planning [9]. An embodied extension, V-JEPA 2, uses an action-conditioned latent predictor in a closed-loop image-goal planning setup; its authors report tabletop robot reaching, grasping, and pick-and-place trials in two labs, while noting that the evidence is limited to those tasks and settings [10]. Evidence from the cited benchmarks and deployments remains bounded: curated prompts, particular models, and specific simulated or real-world tasks do not establish reliable performance in arbitrary environments [6][9][7][8][10].

## Evaluation: realism, prediction, and utility are different targets
Evaluation should be matched to the intended use. Control returns assess task achievement in interactive RL; representation benchmarks assess downstream recognition or transfer; physical-video tests assess whether predicted continuations obey selected physical regularities; and world-model benchmarks can separately score instruction following and physical adherence [1][4][5][6][9]. These outcomes answer different questions and should not be collapsed into a single leaderboard. V-JEPA’s reported classification results, for example, do not on their own demonstrate reliable action planning, just as high video fidelity does not establish physical correctness [5][6][9].

Generalization tests are particularly important. In the cited mechanics study, success on familiar distributions did not carry over to out-of-distribution scenarios [7]. Benchmark work that varies physical laws and dynamic conditions broadens evaluation beyond image quality, but the curated nature of these tests means they remain partial measures [6][9][7][8]. A defensible assessment therefore combines prediction quality with intervention or action tests, novel conditions, and downstream utility, while clearly stating the task and data regime [6][9][7].

## Trends and open problems
Recent work represented here is moving from video plausibility toward physical and causal prediction, explicit controllability, and task-relevant evaluation [6][9][7][8][2]. The open question is whether larger or more richly trained passive predictors will acquire general physical abstractions, or whether interactive experience and action-conditioned objectives are needed; the available studies do not settle this question [6][7][8]. Architectures also face a core trade-off: a model trained to preserve visual detail may spend capacity on appearance that is irrelevant to action, whereas an abstract latent may omit information needed for accurate downstream control [1][4][5].

Important limitations remain. Reported results are often tied to particular task suites, model families, or curated physics benchmarks, and different papers use incompatible metrics [1][4][6][9][7][8]. Generalization under distribution shift, long-horizon consistency, causal response to interventions, and usefulness for downstream decisions remain distinct questions rather than established capabilities [9][7][2]. The evidence therefore supports treating world models as a family of predictive and decision-support methods, and evaluating each against its intended use rather than assuming that visual realism or a strong result on one benchmark guarantees a general-purpose simulator [6][9][7][2].
